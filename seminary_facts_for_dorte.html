Seminary at Strawberry

Facts & Sources — A point-by-point rebuttal with direct citations to every source

Prepared March 1, 2026

1
The "Approved Plan"
```
Claim #1
"Maybe next time you move to an area, don't move next to a site that has an approved plan you don't agree with."
There is no approved plan. The 1984 Master Plan expired in 2017.
The Marin County Board of Supervisors voted unanimously not to extend the 1984 Master Plan. It lapsed at the end of 2017. North Coast's current proposal is a brand-new application that requires a new Master Plan AND amendments to the Strawberry Community Plan. Nobody moved next to an "approved project" — there is a pending application under environmental review.
Sources
Mill Valley Patch, Dec. 13, 2017 — "Board Upholds Denial Of Strawberry Seminary Proposal" Marin County CDA — P1490 Official Project Page
The 1984 Plan restricted housing to students, faculty & staff only.
The Strawberry Community Plan and the 1984 Master Plan both describe the on-site housing as exclusively for "students, faculty and staff" of the seminary. North Coast's application explicitly requests a Community Plan Amendment to remove that restriction — which itself proves that general market-rate housing was never part of any approved plan.
Sources
Marin County CDA — P1490: amendment needed so units "would no longer be exclusively dedicated to students, faculty and staff" Strawberry Community Association — confirms Community Plan language
Riley Hurd's argument: the 1953 CUP was voided by the 1984 Plan.
The 1984 Master Plan stated: "With approval of this Master Plan, the previously approved 1959 Campus Plan Use Permit shall become null and void." Don Dickenson, who was the county's projects planner when the 1984 plan was written, publicly agrees with this interpretation.
Sources
Marin IJ, July 20, 2024 — quotes the 1984 Master Plan language and Dickenson's agreement Marin IJ, Sept. 15, 2024 — Dickenson: "At that time, the assumption was that the master plan was replacing the conditional use permit"
```
2
"These Aren't New Units"
```
Claim #2
"These aren't new units, they are replacing existing units that have people living in them right now."
197 units are net-new — no predecessor on the site. Plus 150 senior apartments.
The Planning Commission Staff Report states: "A total of 336 residential units are proposed, of which 139 units would replace existing units." Here is the math:
    336 proposed residential units
– 139 replacing existing units
= 197 net-new residential units
+ 150 senior care apartments (entirely new facility)
= 347 net-new units on a site with 211 seminary dorm rooms
That is not "replacing existing units." That is more than doubling the unit count — with a fundamentally different use.
Sources
Marin County Planning Commission Staff Report — "of which 139 units would replace existing units" Marin County Official Press Release, Dec. 16, 2025 — project description
The current tenant (Olivet University) has ~120 students and 15 faculty.
The seminary relocated in 2014–2016. Since then, Olivet University has operated on a lease with approximately 120 students and 15 faculty members. The campus was built for up to 1,000 students; it is operating at roughly 12% of that capacity. The site is not a thriving residential community — it is a substantially underutilized former seminary campus.
Sources
Marin IJ, Feb. 18, 2020 — "North Coast currently leases it to Olivet University, which has approximately 120 students and 15 faculty members" Marin County — "Golden Gate Baptist Seminary had an enrollment of about 600 students in 2015 when its leadership decided to close it permanently"
The existing housing was always restricted to students, faculty & staff — not the general public.
The Strawberry Community Plan and the 1984 Master Plan both describe on-site housing as exclusively for "students, faculty and staff." North Coast must amend the Community Plan to remove this restriction — which itself proves market-rate housing was never part of any approved plan. Equating a seminary dorm room with a market-rate home is, as SNA attorney Riley Hurd put it, comparing fundamentally different uses on "the impact scale."
Sources
Marin County CDA — P1490: amendment needed so units "would no longer be exclusively dedicated to students, faculty and staff" Strawberry Community Association — confirms Community Plan language
```
3
"100s of Families for Decades"
```
Claim #3
"The Seminary has had 100s of families living there for decades."
Maximum physical housing capacity ever built: 211 units.
The original plan called for housing for ~400 people, but the developer's own website states "a large portion of which was never built." The 211 units that were built were seminary dorms and small family apartments — not typical residential homes.
Sources
TheSeminaryAtStrawberry.com — Historical Uses (developer's own site)
Enrollment declined since the 1970s. Most students commuted — they didn't live on campus.
Peak enrollment was 750–910 students (sources vary by decade), but the developer's own website states: "Most of the campus population lived in the surrounding community and commuted to the site." Enrollment was declining for 40+ years before the seminary left.
Sources
TheSeminaryAtStrawberry.com — Historical Uses (developer's own site) Mill Valley Historical Society — "Enrollment... had been declining since the 1970's" Marin Magazine, 2019 — "Enrollment peaked in the 1970s at 750 students"
The seminary left in 2014–2016. Olivet had only ~120 students.
The Golden Gate Baptist Seminary trustees voted to relocate in April 2014. The Mill Valley campus officially closed June 3, 2016. When Olivet University took over the lease, it had approximately 100–120 students and 15 faculty members. The Southern Baptist Convention itself said housing near Mill Valley was "extremely difficult, undermining any meaningful growth strategy."
Sources
Gateway Seminary — "Golden Gate Seminary closes Mill Valley campus" (June 3, 2016) Gateway Seminary — Official Seminary History page Marin IJ, Feb. 18, 2020 — Olivet had ~120 students, 15 faculty Baptist Press — SBC Executive Committee: housing "extremely difficult"
```
4
"Traffic Issues Are Grossly Inflated"
```
Claim #4
"I believe that the traffic issues are grossly inflated."
The EIR cost $1.64 MILLION and was prepared by Ascent Environmental, Inc.
The 684-page Environmental Impact Report was prepared by Ascent Environmental, a professional CEQA consulting firm, under contract approved by the Marin County Board of Supervisors. Trip generation uses the ITE Trip Generation Manual — the industry-standard reference compiled from thousands of empirical traffic studies nationwide. This is the same methodology used for every major development project in California.
Sources
Marin IJ, July 20, 2024 — "North Coast picked up the $1.64 million tab for producing the study" Press Democrat, Sept. 16, 2024 — identifies Curtis Alling, project principal at Ascent Environmental Planning Commission Staff Report — confirms Ascent Environmental contract
Parisi Transportation Consultants: 1,600 → 2,297 → 4,323 daily trips.
Parisi Transportation Consultants, an independent Bay Area traffic engineering firm, was engaged by the Seminary Tomorrow community working group. Critically, this study was paid for by North Coast (the developer) as part of the collaborative process — not by project opponents. Parisi's findings: seminary-era operations generated ~1,600 daily trips; current Olivet-era use ~2,297 daily trips; the 234-unit proposal would generate ~4,323 daily trips.
Sources
TheSeminaryAtStrawberry.com — Seminary Tomorrow Group (developer's own site confirms Parisi) Marin IJ, Feb. 18, 2020 — reports the 1,600 / 2,297 / 4,323 daily trip figures from Parisi study
A THIRD firm (Panorama Environmental) peer-reviewed the EIR — and said it UNDERSTATES impacts.
The Seminary Neighborhood Association hired Panorama Environmental to conduct an independent peer review. Their finding: the EIR contained "insufficient traffic analysis" — meaning the professional critics think the EIR understates, not overstates, the traffic impacts.
Sources
Marin IJ, Sept. 15, 2024 — "SNA hired Panorama Environmental to conduct a comprehensive peer review"
And NONE of this fully accounts for the proposed 1,000-student school.
All of the traffic numbers above are for the residential component. On top of that, North Coast is reserving the right to operate a 1,000-student college/university on the site. Commuting students, faculty, and staff would generate their own daily vehicle trips — as would employees of the proposed senior care facility, preschool, and fitness center.

Multiple Planning Commissioners criticized the EIR for failing to analyze the school's impacts. Commissioner Don Dickenson: "I think it's impossible to do analysis of an institution when you don't know what it is." SNA attorney Riley Hurd: "What kind of school? What time does it open? How do people get there? How could there possibly be a meaningful EIR without that data?" North Coast has refused to specify what kind of institution it would be.
Sources
Marin IJ, Sept. 15, 2024 — Commissioners and attorney quotes on unanalyzed school impacts Marin IJ, July 20, 2024 — "North Coast is reserving the right to operate a 1,000-student college/university" Marin IJ, Jan. 2, 2026 — "North Coast intends to locate a graduate-level academic institution at the Seminary"
```
